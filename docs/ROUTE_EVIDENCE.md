## What the lines establish

Primary geometry is the [USDA National Forest System Trails layer](https://services9.arcgis.com/5rkKDrGuFJxI7V5b/arcgis/rest/services/USFS_National_Forest_System_Trails/FeatureServer/0), supplemented inside Devils Postpile by [NPS Public Trails](https://mapservices.nps.gov/arcgis/rest/services/NationalDatasets/NPS_Public_Trails/FeatureServer/0). Routes follow graph edges along agency coordinates. Lake-to-lake straight lines were not substituted for trails. OpenStreetMap was a cross-check, not mixed into calculated route geometry.

Distances use haversine lengths and representative trail vertices near lakes. They are approximate, rounded, and include retracing. Individually rounded day values can differ from rounded trip totals. Campsite approaches, shoreline travel, elevation and detours add distance. Cumulative ascent has not been calculated; a 5,000-foot daily ascent ceiling is **not verified**.

Small cartographic endpoint joins contribute to graph distance but are omitted from rendered geometry. New-route rendered gaps can reach approximately 23 metres. One NPS/USFS boundary gap on the JMT is approximately 59 metres. These are source-topology correspondences, not proof of a missing bridge or currently passable ground. Place pins are approximate basin or feature references, **not designated legal campsites or navigation waypoints**.

Agency inventory proves mapped alignment, not current maintenance, open access, reliable signage or a terrain class. Established hiking trails are the intended dry-condition choice within the user's Class 2 limit. Snow, ice, washed-out tread or obstacles can invalidate that assumption; do not add a scramble around an obstruction. Historical photos and forecasts do not prove current footing. Minaret focus is shorter and more retreatable, not certified safe in adverse weather.

## Construction and parking

The signed [2026 Reds Meadow order 05-04-52-26-19](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/inyo/alerts/reds-meadow-road-reconstruction-project) is effective June 3–December 31, 2026. During September 21–November 1 it closes the entirety of road 03S11 and National Forest land within 200 feet on either side from Sunday 11 p.m. through Saturday 7 a.m. October 13–16 falls within the weekday closure. A normal wilderness permit is not the order's specific FS-7700-48 exemption.

The common Horseshoe approach follows Mammoth Pass → Upper Crater Meadow cutoff → PCT/JMT → Minaret Creek, rather than the direct resort approach. Comparison with [USFS EDW RoadBasic 03S11](https://apps.fs.usda.gov/arcx/rest/services/EDW/EDW_RoadBasic_01/MapServer/0), sampled every five metres or less, found minimum separation approximately **342 metres / 1,122 feet**, with no sampled route inside the 200-foot corridor. This supports avoiding this particular road closure. It is not surveyed boundary evidence, ranger authorization, or clearance of every possible closure.

Horseshoe Lake overnight parking for October 13–16 and Lake Mary Road vehicle access remain unresolved. A published parking lot is not permission to leave a vehicle overnight. [Forest parking order 05-04-50-25-17](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/inyo/alerts/stay-limits-parking-and-developed-recreation-site-use) prohibits parking contrary to posted instructions. Current signs control; a wilderness permit does not override them. Ask Mammoth Ranger Station **760-924-5500** for exact spaces, road access and the named trail approach; town roads contact is **760-934-8054**. No ranger phone confirmation occurred in this session.

[NPS hours](https://www.nps.gov/depo/planyourvisit/hours.htm) distinguish monument trails from road access and prohibit valley/monument overnight parking after October 15. That rule does not itself establish Horseshoe Lake parking permission. The Reds shuttle's last 2026 service was October 11; do not offer it for this trip.

Sabrina's [official brochure](https://www.fs.usda.gov/media/123424) distinguishes the overnight turnout near the North Lake junction from day-use road-end parking. Road walking is additional to mapped trail totals. Every alternative needs its own current parking, road, terrain and weather checks.

## Permits, water and camps

**Nothing is reserved or paid.** No reservation number or issued permit exists. [Recreation.gov permit 233262](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/233262) is the authoritative booking page. For one person entering October 13, the October 11, 2026, 11:14 p.m. PDT snapshot showed AA16 15/15, AA05 29/30, JM01 29/30, JM05 15/15 and JM17 23/25, with Book Now enabled. A subsequent AA16 check showed 15 available and a login requirement. These observations are historical snapshots, **not current availability**. Check again before checkout. The matching entry permit governs wilderness travel under its terms; it does not grant parking, closed-road passage or safe conditions. Recreation.gov can allow an exit whose road is closed.

Lake water is expected, but current flow, ice and shoreline access are unverified. Treat water, carry adequate capacity and backup treatment, and protect a wet filter from freezing according to its manufacturer's instructions. A filter alone does not establish feasibility.

Camp locations are lake areas, not assured pads. The [wilderness restrictions order](https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r05/inyo/publication/alerts/05-04-50-25-02%20Wilderness%20Use%20Restrictions.pdf) includes specific setbacks and exclusions around Garnet, Thousand Island, Ediza, Shadow, Duck and Purple lakes. Read the effective order and exhibits before selecting a site; lake-center pins do not encode those boundaries.
